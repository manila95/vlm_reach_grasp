The idea: split the task where learning matters

1 · Ground

A local VLM finds the target

RGB-D object proposals plus zoomed yes/no verification turn the instruction into a 3-D target. Zero-shot, no task-specific training.

2 · Reach

A planned move goes to a handoff pose

The handoff pose sits just before contact, as an offset learned from demos in the target’s frame. It works at any location.

3 · Manipulate

A small policy learns only the local part

The policy uses egocentric inputs (wrist camera, pose relative to the handoff) and learns only the contact-rich phase: grasp, insert, open, place.

Imitation then has to capture only the variance of the final manipulation, not where things are in the scene.

The idea

Three components. The VLM handles “which object”, the planned reach handles “where it is”, and the learned policy handles only the last centimetres of contact-rich manipulation. The claim is that this split generalizes across layouts where end-to-end policies, which also have to learn where things are, collapse.
