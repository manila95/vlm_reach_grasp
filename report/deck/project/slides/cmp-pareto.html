Reading the trade-off: the fastest system is ours with Qwen-8B (7.2 s, a tie with π0.5 in accuracy). Moving the VLM up the size ladder buys accuracy at the price of grounding time: Qwen-32B adds about 2 s and 2.7 points, Gemini Flash with recursive querying adds about 9 s and 5.7 points, relative to Qwen-8B. All three of our systems share the same 0.14 s policy and the same robot motion. Two things are deliberately left out of the time totals: our grounding is serial here (the robot waits), but it only needs the first image, so a real deployment could run it while the arm moves toward the workspace; and the VLAs are measured with blocking inference, while their servers can overlap chunk inference with execution. A dev-split run of Gemini 4 Argon with numbered marks (offline grounding accuracy 98.6%) takes 3.6 s per scene instead of 9.9 s, so a faster API model may move the Gemini point left; it has no test-split run yet.
